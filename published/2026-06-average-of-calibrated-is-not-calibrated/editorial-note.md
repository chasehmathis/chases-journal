# Editorial note

*Chase's Journal*, June 2026 issue — selected for publication.

The board chose this note as the inaugural issue's winner because it is the rare
submission that is at once completely correct, fully reproducible, genuinely
novel in its constants, and organized around a real idea. It takes a qualitative
folklore fact — the average of distinct calibrated forecasters is itself
miscalibrated — and pins it to sharp, memorable numbers (a two-line ceiling of
$1/2$, a closed-form $8/27$ witness with a clean Bayesian story), while surfacing
a conceptual point that outlives the arithmetic: the "worst-case miscalibration of
an ensemble" is not a single number but a function of the reliability diagram's
bin width, so a pooled forecaster can read as perfectly calibrated or maximally
miscalibrated depending only on resolution. Every reported quantity was
re-derived by hand and reproduced exactly by the shipped `sim.py`, and the note is
unusually honest about the boundary between what it proves and what it merely
illustrates — the standard we want this journal to set.
