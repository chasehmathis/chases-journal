# Editorial decision — *Chase's Journal*, June 2026

**Issue decided:** 2026-07-01 · **Submissions reviewed:** 9 (`submissions/2026-06/m1`–`m9`)
**Published:** 1 · **Revise-and-resubmit:** 3 · **Declined:** 5

This is the journal's inaugural issue. The board convened three reviewers with
deliberately different priorities — **Reviewer T** (theory/rigor, re-derives the
central claim), **Reviewer S** (significance/novelty, cross-checks against the
literature), **Reviewer C** (clarity/reproducibility, re-runs every `sim.py`) —
plus the **editor-in-chief**, who weighs them and makes the call. Every
theoretical claim was independently re-derived; every computational note had its
code re-executed and its reported numbers checked against actual output; novelty
was checked against arXiv and the open web.

The headline finding of the review is that this was a **strong and remarkably
even field**: eight of nine notes are mathematically correct, cleanly written,
and reproduce their reported numbers exactly. In such a field the deciding axes
become **novelty**, **the reliability of the artifact as shipped**, and whether
the note is built around a genuine idea rather than a repackaging of folklore.
Scores are 1–5 (reproducibility is folded into Correctness for computational
claims).

## Winner: **m2 — "Averaging perfectly calibrated forecasters: a sharp 8/27, a tight 1/2, and the resolution in between"** (Søren Bachmann)

**Reviewer T (rigor).** Re-derived all three results independently. *Fact 0*
(a conditional expectation is calibrated) is the standard tower-rule argument.
*Proposition 1* (ceiling ECE ≤ 1/2): Jensen (conditional) → triangle inequality
over convex weights → calibration gives E[|Y−Pₖ| | Pₖ=v] = 2v(1−v) ≤ 1/2. Every
step valid, bound tight at v=1/2. *Theorem 2* (the 8/27 witness): the agents
E[Y|A], E[Y|B] for Y = A∨B each have ECE 0; the equal-weight average has
ECE = 2q(1−q)², maximized at q=1/3 to give exactly 8/27 ≈ 0.2963; the three-row
mass×gap table is arithmetically exact. *Proposition 3* (sup = 1/2, approached not
attained): the checkerboard 2×2 construction's row/column values match the stated
formulas; four distinct averages → 1/2, masses → 1/4, giving pop-ECE → 1/2. The
subtle sup-vs-max distinction is handled correctly. **No error found; 5/5.**

**Reviewer S (novelty).** The *qualitative* backbone — a nontrivial linear pool
of distinct calibrated forecasters is uncalibrated and underconfident — is
Ranjan & Gneiting (2010), with the extremizing fix from Satopää et al. (2014) /
Baron et al. (2014); the binning sensitivity of ECE is Guo et al. (2017),
Vaicenavičius et al. (2019), Kumar et al. (2019). All are cited honestly. What
does **not** appear in the literature as a named result is the binary-outcome
*quantification*: the exact 1/2 ceiling, the closed-form 8/27 witness, and — the
genuine conceptual contribution — the **resolution-indexed** worst case, i.e. the
observation that "worst-case ensemble miscalibration" is a *function of bin
width*, with resolution-robust witnesses (spread reports) cleanly separated from
resolution-fragile ones (reports clustering at the base rate). Modest but real
new content. **3/5** — the highest novelty in the field, tied with m9.

**Reviewer C (reproducibility).** `python3 sim.py` runs clean and regenerates all
three figures. Every printed number matches `note.md`: the 8/27 value and its
10-bin robustness; the p-sweep 0.306 → 0.500 for the 1/2 construction and the
fixed-bin collapse (p=0.01: pop 0.498, 20-bin 0.000, 100-bin 0.005); the m-agent
climb 0.296 → 0.470 (m=2…12) with population and 20-bin ECE coinciding; the
finite-sample 0.2965 vs population 0.2963 with each agent ≤ 0.001. **No
discrepancies; 5/5.**

**Editor-in-chief.** This note is the cleanest embodiment of what the journal
says it wants: a genuine conceptual core (miscalibration of the linear pool is a
resolution-dependent quantity, not a single number), proved results with sharp
closed-form constants, and a simulation that verifies *every* constant to the
reported precision — the preferred theory-tied-to-simulation shape, executed
without a single caveat on rigor or reproducibility. It also lands squarely in a
recurring interest of the journal (calibration / forecast aggregation). Against
its only novelty-peer, m9, it wins on reliability: m9's artifact does not run to
completion as shipped and one of its theorems rests on a numerically-supported
sketch, whereas m2 has no such gap. For an inaugural issue whose job is to set
the standard, the note that is simultaneously novel, correct, *and* completely
reproducible is the right choice. **Selected for publication.**

## Revise-and-resubmit

### m9 — "Correlation collapses the effective dimension of multiple testing" (Mira Estrada) · Corr 4 / Nov 3 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

The strongest near-miss and, on raw ambition, the most mathematically novel note
in the field: two closed-form exponents for equicorrelated Gaussian multiple
testing — FWER decaying as m^(−β(ρ)) with β(ρ) = (1−√(1−ρ))²/ρ, and effective
multiplicity growing as m^(1−ρ) — where the prior literature (Das & Bhandari;
Dey & Bhandari) had only convergence-to-zero and the α(1−ρ) bound. Reviewer T
verified Lemma 1 (against brute-force MC), the z₀ computation driving Theorem 2,
the algebraic identity for β, and Theorem 3's EVT argument; the exponents are not
in doubt. **Two concrete blockers kept it out of this issue, both fixable:**

1. **Reproducibility.** `sim.py` does not terminate in reasonable time as shipped
   (the Result-2 loop inverts the quadrature up to m=10⁹ for three ρ, plus a
   5×10⁴/200k-rep MC row — collectively >500 s). Cap or subsample the Result-2
   m-range (≤ ~10⁷) so a reader can re-run it end to end. The printed numbers
   that *did* complete match the note exactly, and the runner-up status is
   entirely on our confidence in the math, not the code's veracity — but a
   theory-led journal that promises "a reader should be able to re-run it" cannot
   publish an artifact that stalls.
2. **Rigor.** The §3.2 integral-term Laplace estimate (the boundary-maximum
   claim) is a numerically-supported *sketch*, not a bound with uniform remainder
   control. Either upgrade it to a rigorous argument, or restate Theorem 2 as
   resting on the rigorous tail-term half plus the numerics and label it
   accordingly (the honesty is already there in Limitation v; make it structural).

Address both and this is a clear accept in a future issue — quite possibly a
winner. Optional: a sentence delimiting scope against the sparse-ρ regime.

### m3 — "The winner's curse is a selection problem, not an estimation problem" (Priya Venkataraman) · Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

A correct, elegant, and beautifully written reframing: the split estimator's MSE
for the *selected* arm's own mean is exactly 1/(1−p), invariant to the number of
arms, the configuration, and selection quality, so the winner's-curse difficulty
lives entirely in the *selection* account. Reviewer T verified all three
propositions; the sim matches exactly. **Blocker: novelty/positioning.** The
selected-vs-best-arm distinction and the regime-dependent crossover are
independently developed in Berman, Zhang & Zhao, "Valuing Winners" (arXiv:
2605.18887, May 2026), which predates this submission and is not cited; the
two-stage conditionally-unbiased estimator is Cohen–Sackrowitz (1989), correctly
credited. To resubmit: engage Berman et al. head-on, and **sharpen the claim to
what is genuinely distinctive** — the *exact* 1/(1−p) invariance and the
"estimation account is trivially cheap" corollary — rather than the crossover,
which is now prior art. Foreground the "different point estimates across δ"-style
observation. With the delta made precise, this becomes a crisp accept.

### m7 — "Adjusting for a noisy confounder removes at most a fraction R of the bias" (Esther Nakamura-Lindqvist) · Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

Impeccable algebra (verified symbolically and by simulation): the exact residual
confounding fraction F = R(1−R²ₓ)/[R(1−R²ₓ)+(1−R)] and the clean corollary
F ≤ R, with equality iff the confounder does not drive treatment. A genuinely
useful practitioner diagnostic (reliability is a *ceiling* on bias removed, not
the amount removed). **Blocker: the nearest prior art is uncited.** The
mechanism and the "proportion of bias removed" framing date to Cochran & Rubin
(1973) and the standard multi-regressor measurement-error econometrics
(Griliches; Bound et al.; Abel). To resubmit: cite and delineate the
contribution against these, and — the path to real novelty — work out the
**vector-confounder generalization** (the scalar R²ₓ becomes a partial-R² matrix
object), which the note flags as open. That extension would lift this from a
sharp repackaging to a new result.

## Declined this issue

All five are **correct, reproducible, and well-crafted** — in a normal month
several would be contenders. They are declined because a monthly issue accepts
exactly one, and each is a clean reframing/quantification of established facts
whose novelty ceiling is intrinsic to the contribution rather than an artifact of
presentation. The board records its respect for the craft in each.

- **m1 — MSE ≠ conformal width (Hana Okabe).** Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5.
  Correct and elegant: split-conformal width is the (1−α)-quantile of |residual|,
  hence insensitive to the tail that dominates MSE; stochastic dominance is the
  right level-free order. All proofs verified, sim matches exactly. The core is an
  essentially elementary corollary of quantile insensitivity, and the CQR
  literature already motivates "don't rank by mean-MSE." *Suggestion for any
  future author:* the stochastic-dominance ordering (Prop 2) is the most
  distinctive piece — develop it against existing stochastic-order results in the
  CP literature and soften the "wrong loss" framing to match the careful body.

- **m4 — Median-of-means not "anytime-in-δ" (Tomás Okabe).** Corr 5 / Nov 2 /
  Sig 3 / Clar 5. A scrupulously honest, self-contained certificate
  (2σ√(k/n)·δ^(−1/k), valid for all δ from one partition) and a clean
  "commit-to-δ-first" reframing. Verified analytically and numerically. The
  underlying facts are folklore (Lugosi–Mendelson) and the deep impossibility is
  DLLO (2016), both cited; the note does not overclaim. Its most novel angle — a
  *fixed* partition yields *different point estimates* at different δ — could be
  foregrounded, and a partial fixed-k lower bound would close the one gap the
  note leans on citation for.

- **m5 — 50/50 is minimax under unknown variance (Priya Venkataraman).**
  Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5. A clean exact identity inf_t ρ(p,t) =
  min(p,1−p), giving equal allocation as the unique maximin design for
  ATE-estimation efficiency; correctly distinguished from the best-arm-ID minimax
  literature. Verified in full. The math is elementary and the factor-of-two is
  textbook; the reframing is sharp but essentially complete as stated, leaving
  little revision headroom. Worth stating explicitly that the value 1/2 is a
  limiting infimum never attained, and that the inner game has no interior saddle.

- **m8 — KL is the wrong divergence for importance sampling (Tomás Errázuriz).**
  Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5. Technically flawless and a lovely cautionary
  example: for q=N(0,1), p=N(0,σ²) the weights have finite variance iff σ² < 2,
  with the exact 1/(σ√(2−σ²)), while KL glides below 0.16 nats — and σ²=2 is
  exactly the Pareto-k̂=1/2 boundary. But every ingredient is established (Agapiou
  et al.; Chatterjee–Diaconis; Owen; Vehtari et al.); the contribution is
  synthesis and framing, which the note states plainly. Excellent as pedagogy;
  below the new-result bar this month.

- **m6 — Bootstrap of the maximum is geometric (R. Følner-Marsh).** Corr 5 /
  Nov 2 / Sig 2 / Clar 5. A model of reproducible exposition: the rank deficit
  converges to a deterministic Geometric(1−e⁻¹) while the value deficit is an
  irreducibly random compound-geometric — "a deterministic count composed with a
  random metric." All derivations verified; sim matches. But every load-bearing
  fact is classical (Bickel–Freedman inconsistency; the 1−e⁻¹ atom; Rényi
  spacings; m-out-of-n repair), and the contribution is packaging. To return as a
  research note it needs a genuinely new theorem — e.g. the general-endpoint
  value law, a data-driven optimal m, or a rigorous random-measure
  characterization of the part-2 limit.
