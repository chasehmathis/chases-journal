# Editorial decision — *Chase's Journal*, July 2026

**Issue decided:** 2026-08-01 · **Submissions reviewed:** 8 (`submissions/2026-07/m1`–`m8`)
**Published:** 1 · **Revise-and-resubmit:** 4 · **Declined:** 3

The board convened three reviewers with deliberately different priorities —
**Reviewer T** (theory/rigor, re-derives the central claim), **Reviewer S**
(significance/novelty, cross-checks against the literature), **Reviewer C**
(clarity/reproducibility, re-runs every `sim.py`) — plus the
**editor-in-chief**, who weighs them and makes the call. Every theoretical
claim was re-derived independently; every note's code was executed and its
reported numbers checked against actual output; novelty was checked against
arXiv and the open web. Two forward-dated citations flagged as suspicious
(m4's arXiv:2603.22668 and m8's arXiv:2603.27414) were verified to be **real**
papers, so no fabrication penalty applies.

The field was strong and even on craft — **all eight notes are correct on what
they prove, cleanly written, and reproduce their reported numbers to the digits
shown** (several run slowly; see below). In such a field the deciding axes are
**the depth of the mathematical core**, **how much of the headline is actually
*proved* versus simulated or borrowed**, and **breadth**. Scores are 1–5
(reproducibility folded into Correctness for computational claims).

A recurring pattern this month deserves naming, because it drove several
verdicts: a number of notes take a genuinely known phenomenon and give it a
*sharp, quotable closed form* — a fine thing to do, but a theory-led journal
must ask whether the closed form is **new** or a **rediscovery**. Three notes
foundered exactly there (m3, m4, m5). The winner is the one note whose central
result is a **proved theorem with no located prior art** and whose significance
is highest.

## Winner: **m1 — "Adjusting for a mismeasured confounder is safe; adjusting for two is not: an exact residual-bias formula and a reliability-homogeneity dichotomy"** (Esther Nakamura-Lindqvist)

This note is the sequel the board *asked for*. In June, m7 ("Adjusting for a
noisy confounder removes at most a fraction $R$ of the bias") was sent back with
a specific invitation: work out the vector-confounder case, where the scalar
reliability $R_X^2$ becomes a matrix object. m1 takes up that invitation and, in
doing so, **reverses its own predecessor's headline** — which is exactly the
kind of payoff the revise-and-resubmit loop exists to produce.

**Reviewer T (rigor).** Re-derived every result from scratch. *Theorem 1* (the
exact naive/proxy biases with $K=\Sigma_U-\Sigma_U\Sigma_W^{-1}\Sigma_U=
\Sigma_U\Sigma_W^{-1}\Sigma_\delta$): the Schur/block-inverse step is correct —
the bias is the $X$-coordinate of the population regression of the confounding
term $b^\top U$ on $(X,W)$, the top row of $\Sigma_Z^{-1}$ is
$(1/S,\,-\tfrac1S c^\top\Sigma_W^{-1})$, and the numerator collapses to $a^\top K
b$ via $I-\Sigma_W^{-1}\Sigma_U=\Sigma_W^{-1}\Sigma_\delta$; the PSD argument and
the $k=1$ reduction to June's note both check. *Theorem 2* (the dichotomy): **both
directions verified.** (b)⇒(a) is direct; (a)⇒(b) is the elegant step — the
$s\to\infty$ limit forces $|a^\top K b|\le|a^\top\Sigma_U b|$ for all $a,b$, so
$a^\top\Sigma_U b=0\Rightarrow a^\top Kb=0$, hence $Kb\parallel\Sigma_U b$ for
every $b$, so $K\Sigma_U^{-1}=\lambda I$, i.e. $K=\lambda\Sigma_U$, unwinding to
$\Sigma_\delta=\tfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\Sigma_U$. *Corollary 3* (amplify /
reverse / manufacture): all three closed-form witnesses reproduce analytically
and numerically (manufacture $B_{\text{naive}}=0,\,B_W=-1/3$; amplification ratio
$\approx 28$, unbounded as the residual naive bias $\to 0$). **No error found;
5/5.** One fair observation: necessity uses only the non-amplification half of
"safe," so the sign clause in (a) is implied — a one-line remark, not a flaw.

**Reviewer S (novelty).** The *closed form* of Theorem 1 is standard
multivariate errors-in-variables / Frisch–Waugh algebra, with the reliability
matrix $\Lambda=\Sigma_U\Sigma_W^{-1}$ being exactly Gleser's object — and the
note says so, citing Gleser, Carroll et al., and the several-regressors
literature honestly. That the qualitative "adjustment can worsen bias" is
possible is known (Ogburn–VanderWeele 2013 for single/ordinal confounders; Zoh
et al. 2024). **What is genuinely new, and what the board could not locate
anywhere, is the sharp *iff*:** universal safety over *all* effect
configurations holds precisely when $\Sigma_\delta\propto\Sigma_U$ — a necessity
statement quantifying over all $(a,b,s)$ at once — together with the clean
three-mode taxonomy and, in particular, the "manufacture" mode (a naive
estimator that is *exactly unbiased* is pushed off zero by adjustment).
**3/5** — moderate, but new where it counts, and the vector generalization is a
non-trivial, correct answer to the journal's own open question.

**Reviewer C (reproducibility).** `python3 sim.py` runs to completion in ≈2
minutes and every printed number matches `note.md`: the closed-form-vs-direct
max discrepancy $1.47\times10^{-14}$; the safety scan ($-9.75\times10^{-8}$
homogeneous, $+2.356$ heterogeneous with a sign reversal); and all five rows of
the theory-vs-Monte-Carlo scenario table (scalar, homogeneous, manufacture,
reversal, amplify) to three digits, with every oracle bias zero to MC error.
The runtime is dominated by a large finite-sample table ($n=2\times10^5$, 40
reps, 3 regressions × 5 scenarios); the load-bearing theory checks (T1, T2) print
first. This is *re-runnable* — materially different from June's m9, which did
not terminate. **5/5**, with a minor-revision note to add a fast mode.

**Editor-in-chief.** In a field where every note is correct and every note
reproduces, the tie-breakers are depth and novelty-where-it-counts, and m1 wins
both. It is the only submission whose headline is a **proved theorem with no
located prior art** (the $\Sigma_\delta\propto\Sigma_U$ dichotomy), it carries
the **highest significance score** in the field (a memorable, actionable
correction to the reflex "I controlled for it, so it's handled"), and its
mathematical core — the substitution "adjustment replaces the confounding
geometry $\Sigma_U$ by $K$," and the eigenvector argument pinning safety to a
single geometric condition — is genuine theory, with the simulation confirmatory
rather than load-bearing. It also embodies the journal's process working as
designed: a June revise-and-resubmit that came back and *overturned* its own
scalar result. On breadth: only one issue has been published to date
(calibration, June), so causal inference / measurement error is not a repeat,
and the $m7\to m1$ lineage is the intended R&R mechanism, not topic-orbiting.
**Selected for publication.**

## Revise-and-resubmit

### m2 — "The degrees-of-freedom tax on blocking" (Aurélie Vandenberghe) · Corr 5 / Nov 3 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

The strongest near-miss, and accept-quality on craft: a correct, fast-running,
cleanly written note that turns the classical "blocking wastes degrees of
freedom" folklore into a sharp $\alpha$-aware threshold $\rho^\*(g)=1-
(t_{N-2}/t_{N-g-1})^2$, a memorable $(z^2{+}1)/4(n{-}1)$ pairing rule, and the
counterintuitive monotonicity that *finer* blocking carries the *heaviest* tax.
Reviewer T verified the variance identities, the Schur/half-width inequality,
the Cornish–Fisher expansion, and Prop 2; every number in Table 1 and the
simulation reproduces exactly. It lost to m1 only on depth and significance —
the author accurately calls it "a sharpening and reframing, not a new
estimator." **To convert to an accept:** (1) the interval half-width iff is
exact but the *power* ordering is only leading-order (noncentral-$t$ power
depends on the df beyond the critical-value ratio) — back the "more power" clause
with a direct noncentral-$t$ check or state the caveat sharply; (2) report
Monte-Carlo standard errors on the empirical crossings (0.142 / 0.153) so the
$\mathbb{E}[s]=\sigma c_\nu$ correction is a *match*, not a visible 0.006 gap;
(3) cite Imai (2008) on matched-pair precision and state precisely what the
$t$-quantile framing adds beyond the classical Fisher/Cochran–Cox RE
df-correction; (4) note the Cornish–Fisher error growth at $n=3$ (abs err 0.022)
and restrict the advertised accuracy to $n\gtrsim 5$. This is a clear accept in a
future issue, and it opens ground (experimental design) the journal has not yet
published.

### m8 — "Power-tuning is not free: an exact loss factor for prediction-powered inference" (Júlia Renard) · Corr 5 / Nov 3 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

Correct, topical, and genuinely useful: the reframing "PPI++ power-tuning $K$
predictors $=$ the fitted intercept of an OLS regression of $Y$ on the $K$
predictions" yields the exact loss factor $L(n,K)=(n-2)/(n-K-2)$, the inclusion
threshold $g_K>1/(n-K-1)$, a finite optimal $K^\*$, and a $t$-interval coverage
fix. Reviewer T re-derived Theorem 1 (inverse-Wishart mean → $K/(n(n-K-2))$) and
Proposition 2 exactly; every reported number reproduces (the full script exceeds
a 180 s budget at shipped reps — lower the defaults or add a fast mode). **Two
things keep it out of this issue, both fixable:** (1) the loss factor itself is
classical control-variate theory (Lavenberg–Welch), which the note credits — but
more importantly, the *finite-sample-tax / undercoverage* story for $K=1$ is
already in an **uncited** 2025 paper, "No Free Lunch: Non-Asymptotic Analysis of
Prediction-Powered Inference" (arXiv:2505.20178), and "Power Analysis for PPI"
(arXiv:2603.16041) is also relevant; (2) reference [7] (arXiv:2603.27414, the
Multiple-PPI paper) is real but under-specified. **To resubmit:** cite and
differentiate both papers head-on, and foreground the genuinely new pieces — the
exact **$K$-predictor** loss factor, the inclusion threshold, and $K^\*$ as a
*predictor-selection rule* — rather than the tax/undercoverage framing that is
now prior art. With the positioning fixed, this is an attractive accept in a
fresh area (semi-supervised / prediction-powered inference).

### m7 — "Trust the sign, distrust the size" (Livia Marchetti) · Corr 5 / Nov 3 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

Elegant, fully reproducible, and honestly positioned: it makes Gelman & Carlin's
qualitative "sign is safer than size" exact, with a low-power law
$M(d)\sim r(c)/d$ whose slope is the inverse Mills ratio $r(c)=\varphi(c)/
\Phi(-c)=2.338$ at $\alpha=0.05$, and a computable "reliability window" (power
$\in(0.165,0.591)$) where the sign is $>99\%$ reliable while the magnitude is
inflated $>30\%$. Reviewer T verified the closed forms to machine precision and
independently confirmed the constants; the note is *not* too close to June's
winner's-curse m3 (different quantity and mechanism). It reads as an incremental
sharpening rather than a discovery — the constant $r(c)$ is a textbook
truncated-normal mean, and the qualitative separation is Gelman–Carlin's. **To
resubmit:** (1) fix the $\alpha=0.01$ constant, which should be $r(c)=2.8919$,
not 2.8892; (2) temper "sharp separation" to name the actual new content — the
*rate* contrast (Gaussian $\Phi(-c-d)$ vs polynomial $\varphi(c-d)/(d\pi)$) and
the computable edges — and add one sentence distinguishing the contribution from
van Zwet–Cator (Type-M + shrinkage, not the sign contrast); (3) supply the
one-line analytic proof that $M$ is decreasing in $d$ (currently numerical),
which would let "read $M$ off power alone" stand as a theorem.

### m6 — "The hazard rate of the noise governs proxy overoptimization" (Anders Kjeldgaard) · Corr 4 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5

The most *ambitious* reframing in the field and the one that opens the freshest
ground (the statistics of optimization / Goodhart's law): the limiting selected
value $\mathbb{E}[S\mid P=t]$ as $t\to\infty$ is the mean of $S$ exponentially
tilted by the noise's asymptotic hazard rate $\lambda^\*$, giving a *continuum*
— collapse ($\lambda^\*{=}0$), an exact plateau $\mu_S+\sigma_S^2\lambda^\*$
($\lambda^\*\in(0,\infty)$), unbounded gain ($\lambda^\*{=}\infty$) — that
refines the recent benign-vs-catastrophic dichotomy, plus an overoptimization
"hump" corollary. The two endpoints are proved rigorously and every simulated
constant reproduces (a reviewer's independent Gamma(2,1) check even supports the
unproven interior). **But for a theory-led venue the note sells a continuum
whose *interior* — the actual new technical content — it does not prove**
(it is punted to an unstated "domination hypothesis"), and the mechanism
(Gibbs conditioning / exponential tilting; hazard rate → max-domain of
attraction) is classical. **To convert to an accept:** (1) **prove the interior**
$\lambda^\*\in(0,\infty)$ limit — a mild ultimate-monotonicity + Potter-bound
dominated-convergence argument should close it, and this is the paper's real
selling point; (2) sharpen prior-art positioning against the Gibbs-conditioning
literature (Dembo–Zeitouni) and the heavy-tailed conditional-limit result
(arXiv:0912.1516), framing the contribution explicitly as *application + the
plateau constant + the hump*; (3) fix the $\lambda^\*{=}\infty$ framing (it falls
outside the tilt formula and is patched via the exact Gaussian). Prove the
interior and this is a strong accept — quite possibly a winner — in a genuinely
new area for the journal.

## Declined this issue

All three are **correct, reproducible, and beautifully written** — in a thinner
month any of them could contend. They are declined because each headline turns
out to be a *rediscovery or a simulated conjecture* rather than a new proved
result, which is precisely the line a theory-led journal must hold. The board
records its respect for the craft in each and offers concrete paths back.

- **m3 — "Averaging e-values cannot accumulate evidence" (Ludovica Farsetti).**
  Corr 5 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5. A polished, scrupulously honest note: the
  average of i.i.d. likelihood-ratio e-values converges a.s. to the ceiling
  $1+\chi^2(q\Vert p)$ while the product grows at rate $\mathrm{KL}$, giving a
  power reversal at $\alpha^\*=1/(1+\chi^2)$. Every derivation and every printed
  number checks. But the core is **two applications of the SLLN plus the
  textbook identity $\int q^2/p = 1+\chi^2$**, and the substantive framing
  (arithmetic averaging is the admissible dependence-agnostic merge; products
  accumulate) is exactly Vovk–Wang. It reads as excellent pedagogy, not a new
  theorem. *Path back:* use the Vovk–Wang **admissibility** theorem to upgrade
  "averaging cannot accumulate" to "*no* symmetric arbitrary-dependence merge
  accumulates" (currently proved only for linear/convex merges), and add a
  genuinely new load-bearing result — a matching impossibility/lower bound, or
  the e-process/sequential extension. That would make the title's promise a
  theorem rather than a restatement.

- **m5 — "When not to weight: a CV threshold for just averaging" (Elias
  Kandelaars).** Corr 4 / Nov 2 / Sig 3 / Clar 5. Correct (Prop 1 and Theorem 2
  re-derived by hand and by two independent simulations; every number matches)
  and honestly labeled as a leading-order asymptotic. The trouble is novelty:
  the flagship constant $2(k-1)/(k(n-1))$ is the **homoscedastic, equal-df
  specialization of Meier's classical 1953 formula** ("Variance of a Weighted
  Mean," *Biometrics* 9:59–73), $2\sum_i g_i(1-g_i)/\nu_i$ at $g_i=1/k$,
  $\nu_i=n-1$ — a paper the note neither cites nor reconciles with. The durable
  contribution is the scale-free $c_v^2$-vs-threshold framing, but that is a
  two-line corollary of the classical penalty. There is also a cosmetic error:
  $H=1+c_v^2+O(c_v^3)$ in general (the $c_v^3$ term is the third central moment
  of the variances), not $O(c_v^4)$. *Path back:* cite and reconcile Meier,
  rescope the claim from "new constant" to "CV-threshold repackaging," fix Eq.
  (5), and — for genuine new content — derive the *exact* (not leading-order)
  $k\,\mathbb{E}[\sum\hat w_i^2]$ for general $k$ (Nair gives $k=2$).

- **m4 — "The robustness valley of the Cauchy combination test" (Lena
  Ostrowski).** Corr 4 / Nov 2 / Sig 2 / Clar 5. A clean $1$-stability reframing
  of ACAT's independence-exactness (Prop 1) and a correct comonotone-exactness
  endpoint (Prop 3), leading to the reframing that *moderate* correlation, not
  strong, is the finite-sample adversary. Two problems keep it below the bar:
  the surprising endpoint (comonotone exactness) is a **one-line tautology** —
  at $\rho=1$ all $p$-values collapse to one, so $T=C$ — and the headline
  "worst in between" **valley** (strict interior inflation, a single interior
  maximum, the $\rho^\*$ drift) is **established only by simulation**, which the
  note is admirably explicit about but the title and abstract overclaim. The
  effect is also small ($\le 1.7\times$), single-block only, and the recommended
  fix is someone else's boundary-layer CCT (arXiv:2603.22668, verified real).
  *Path back:* prove the interior inflation (a small-$\rho$ tail expansion
  showing $\partial S_m/\partial\rho|_{0^+}>0$, or a rigorous lower bound
  $S_m(\rho)>\alpha$ on $(0,1)$) so the valley becomes a theorem; broaden beyond a
  single equicorrelated Gaussian block; align the title with what is proved; and
  add the $\gamma$-monotonicity result (arXiv:2508.05818) to the positioning.

## Verdict

**Published in the July 2026 issue: "Adjusting for a mismeasured confounder is
safe; adjusting for two is not: an exact residual-bias formula and a
reliability-homogeneity dichotomy"** by Esther Nakamura-Lindqvist
(`m1-mismeasured-confounders-dichotomy`). In a strong, even field of eight
correct and reproducible notes, it is the one whose headline is a *proved
theorem with no located prior art* — the exact $\Sigma_\delta\propto\Sigma_U$
condition for adjustment to be universally safe, and the amplify/reverse/
manufacture taxonomy of how it fails — carrying the field's highest significance
and a genuine mathematical core, in the journal's preferred theory-tied-to-
simulation form. It is also the June revise-and-resubmit loop paying off: an
invited vector-confounder extension that overturned its own scalar predecessor.
Four near-misses (m2, m8, m7, m6) carry concrete revise-and-resubmit invitations
with actionable paths — two of them (m8, m6) into areas the journal has not yet
published.
